About me

A statistician who works across fields

I'm a doctoral student in Applied Statistics and Research Methods at the University of Northern Colorado and an American Statistical Association Graduate Statistician (GStat). I'm trained broadly in statistical theory, modeling, and research design, and I enjoy problems where the data don't cooperate: missing values, complex survey designs, rare outcomes, and large datasets that need serious computing.

My methods research focuses on missing-data theory, prediction and calibration, and knowledge distillation. I test these ideas on large national datasets with millions of records and run the heavy computation on high-performance clusters. As a consultant in UNC's Research Consulting Lab, I help faculty and students across disciplines with study design, survey design, and analysis in R, SAS, SPSS, and Python. My applied work so far spans health, business and finance, education, organizational research, and qualitative inquiry.

Before graduate school I earned a BSc in Statistics at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Ghana and spent two and a half years as a business analyst at Sync Consult in Accra. There I analyzed more than 200 companies for investor readiness and supported a Mastercard Foundation COVID-19 recovery program for small businesses. That work taught me to make statistics useful to whoever has to act on the results, whatever the field.

Youth behavioral health · Methods

Not Asked or Not Answered? Missingness Provenance in Youth Suicide-Attempt Prediction

I coded every predictor value in the combined CDC Youth Risk Behavior Survey (2015–2023) by why it was absent, then compared ten models for past-year and injurious suicide attempts.

  • Provenance-aware indicators gave small, consistent AUROC gains.
  • Gains grew when whole jurisdictions were held out and were not explained by survey recognition.
  • Survey-weighted evaluation with PSU cluster bootstrap and 12 pre-specified sensitivity analyses. BRFSS 2023 ACE data served as a contrasting adult test bed.

Benchmark · Open data

PH-MissBench: Rare-Outcome Prediction Tasks with Real, Agency-Documented Missingness

An open benchmark from NCHS natality public-use files in which every predictor cell carries its NCHS-documented missingness class, so missing-data methods can be compared on real patterns rather than simulated ones.

  • Early preterm birth (<34 weeks) and very low birthweight tasks with a temporal train/test split.
  • Task files frozen and hashed before any baseline was run.
  • Reproduces NCHS published birth totals exactly. Six missing-data baselines with stratified bootstrap intervals.

Maternal & child health · Dissertation

Missingness-Aware Knowledge Distillation (MAKD) for Low Birthweight and Preterm Birth

A gradient-boosted teacher trained on complete U.S. birth-certificate records, distilled into student models that operate under the item missingness each state actually reports.

  • State-specific missingness calibrated from CDC WONDER for 51 jurisdictions.
  • On naturally incomplete 2024 records, the distilled student outperformed mean and iterative imputation, with paired bootstrap intervals excluding zero.
  • Extends my Ghana neonatal risk study to U.S. national data.

Vital statistics · Data product

County Birth-Record Completeness Index (CBRCI), 2016–2024

A reproducible, suppression-aware county measure of birth-certificate item completeness from CDC WONDER, linked to HRSA's Area Health Resources File and CDC PLACES.

  • Suppressed counts treated as interval-censored: bounds, truncated beta-binomial multiple imputation, and interval-censored likelihood.
  • Pre-specified reliability rules decide between a composite score and domain scores.
  • Every released file passes a fail-closed disclosure audit. Validated by simulation and against NCHS state figures.
