Research

Methods · Youth behavioral health

Not Asked or Not Answered? Missingness Provenance in YRBS

Every predictor value in the combined CDC Youth Risk Behavior Survey, 2015–2023, coded by why it was absent: not asked by the site, not on that year's questionnaire, or not answered. I compared ten models for past-year and injurious suicide attempts. Provenance-aware indicators gave small, consistent AUROC gains, and the gains grew when whole jurisdictions were held out. BRFSS 2023 ACE data served as a contrasting adult test bed.

1,008,297 records · 250 site-year surveys · 12 pre-specified sensitivity analyses

Benchmark · Open data

PH-MissBench

An open benchmark of rare-outcome prediction tasks built from NCHS natality public-use files, in which every predictor cell carries its NCHS-documented missingness class. It includes early preterm birth and very low birthweight tasks with a temporal train/test split. Task files were frozen and hashed before any baseline was run, and the build reproduces NCHS's published birth totals exactly.

10.5 million singleton births, 2022–2024 · Tasks frozen v1.0 · MIT code, CC BY 4.0 tasks

Methods · Maternal & child health · Dissertation

Missingness-Aware Knowledge Distillation (MAKD)

A gradient-boosted teacher trained on complete U.S. birth-certificate records, distilled into students that operate under state-specific item missingness calibrated from CDC WONDER for 51 jurisdictions. On naturally incomplete 2024 records, the distilled student outperformed mean and iterative imputation, with paired bootstrap intervals excluding zero.

14.5 million births, 2016–2024 · Low birthweight & preterm birth

Data product · Vital statistics

County Birth-Record Completeness Index

A reproducible, suppression-aware county measure of birth-certificate item completeness from CDC WONDER, linked to HRSA AHRF and CDC PLACES. Suppressed counts are treated as interval-censored, and every released file passes a fail-closed statistical disclosure audit. The index was validated by simulation and against NCHS published state figures.

570 counties · 26.4 million births, 2016–2024 · Python package cbrci (MIT)
